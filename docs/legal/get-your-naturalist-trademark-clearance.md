## 1. The Business (confirmed)

GET YOUR NATURALIST is in use at **getyournaturalist.com**: a two-sided online marketplace ("Expert-led nature travel · worldwide") connecting travelers with **verified naturalist guides, day trips, multi-day tours, cruises, and eco-lodge packages** (birding-first; herping, mammals, marine, botany). Supply side onboards guides, lodges, and tour operators; the platform handles search/booking, holds deposits, and processes payments (Stripe), charging 18% commission on day trips/packages and 15% on multi-day tours. The site operates in English and Spanish.

**Relevant classes:**

- **Class 39** — arranging and booking of travel tours, excursions, and sightseeing/nature tours (core);
- **Class 43** — lodging reservation services (eco-lodge packages);
- **Class 35** — online marketplace services / promoting the tours and services of others; possibly
- **Class 36** — escrow/payment holding, if counsel thinks it worth covering (usually not filed separately at this stage).

**Filing basis:** the platform appears live. If bona fide bookings in U.S. commerce have already occurred, a **Section 1(a) use-based** application is available; otherwise file **Section 1(b) intent-to-use**. Either way, document first-use dates now (dated screenshots, first booking records, Stripe records) — they establish common-law priority and will be needed for the Statement of Use.

## 4. Likelihood-of-Confusion Analysis

### 4.1 GET YOUR GUIDE — the issue that decides this clearance

The name works commercially *because* it echoes GetYourGuide, and that is exactly what creates the legal exposure. Running the key *DuPont* factors honestly:

**Factors favoring GetYourGuide:**

- **Mark similarity in structure and commercial impression.** Identical three-word formula — GET YOUR + [person who guides you] — same cadence, same grammatical construction, same message ("book yourself a [guide-type] here").
- **Service overlap.** Both are online marketplaces where consumers book guided tours and experiences. Nature and wildlife tours are bookable on GetYourGuide today, so the services are not merely related — they intersect.
- **Trade channels and consumers.** Same channel (consumer web/mobile booking platforms), overlapping consumers (travelers).
- **Strength of the senior mark.** GetYourGuide is a famous brand in this exact space, entitled to a broadened scope of protection and with the resources and track record to enforce.
- **Intent.** If the record ever shows the name was chosen *to echo* GetYourGuide, that is affirmative evidence of intent to create an association — one of the most damaging facts in a confusion case. **Practical instruction: never reference GetYourGuide in marketing copy, pitch decks, onboarding materials, or internal naming documents.** (This report flags the issue as a risk assessment; it is not that record.)

**Factors favoring GET YOUR NATURALIST:**

- **The words differ in meaning.** "Naturalist" is not "guide"; it denotes a scientific specialist, and the platform's positioning ("expert-led only, no commodity tours") reinforces a distinct commercial impression.
- **The shared element is descriptive.** "Get your ___" is a common commercial construction, and GetYourGuide's own mark is itself built from descriptive words — its scope against non-identical wording is genuinely arguable.
- **Niche differentiation.** A specialist expert-marketplace (verified naturalists, expedition-grade trips, lodge inventory) versus a mass-market activities OTA; sophisticated birders are careful purchasers of expensive multi-day tours (though day-trip buyers are ordinary consumers).

**Assessment: MODERATE-TO-SUBSTANTIAL risk — genuinely arguable both ways.** Three distinct exposure points, in descending order of likelihood: (1) a USPTO examiner citing a GETYOURGUIDE registration under Section 2(d) — survivable with argument, but not certain; (2) an **opposition** by GetYourGuide during the 30-day publication window — they are exactly the kind of senior user that watches for GET YOUR-formative filings in Class 39; (3) a **demand letter against use** at any time, including after the brand is built — the most expensive scenario, since it threatens a forced rebrand of a live marketplace. None of these outcomes is certain, and coexistence is a defensible position; but the downside if it goes badly lands on a business whose brand equity is the domain and name.

## 5. Registrability (Descriptiveness)

Independent of conflicts, there is a **significant Section 2(e)(1) merely-descriptive risk**: for a service whose entire function is getting the consumer a naturalist, GET YOUR NATURALIST arguably just describes the service. Expect a possible refusal or a required disclaimer of NATURALIST. Response options, in order: (a) argue the slogan is unitary and at most suggestive (it requires a mental step — the service books tours, not people); (b) accept a disclaimer of NATURALIST; (c) amend to the **Supplemental Register** and return for the Principal Register under **Section 2(f)** after ~5 years of substantially exclusive use. Note the tension counsel must manage: descriptiveness arguments that shrink GetYourGuide's scope also shrink this mark's own protectability. A narrow-but-registered mark that keeps the domain safe is a perfectly good commercial outcome here.

## 6. Draft Opinion (for attorney review and signature)

Subject to the limitations in Section 1 and verification of the GETYOURGUIDE U.S. portfolio and the pending NATURALIST applications:

1. **Use** of GET YOUR NATURALIST for an online marketplace booking naturalist guides, nature tours, and eco-lodge stays is **not free of material risk**. The mark itself is unclaimed and the "naturalist" field is crowded and descriptive, but the deliberate structural echo of GET YOUR GUIDE — a famous senior mark for overlapping booking services in the same channel — creates a moderate-to-substantial and genuinely two-sided likelihood-of-confusion question. A challenge by GetYourGuide (opposition or demand letter) is a realistic possibility; the defense is credible but the cost of losing is a rebrand.
2. **Registration** on the Principal Register is **uncertain**: a Section 2(d) citation of GETYOURGUIDE and/or a Section 2(e)(1) descriptiveness refusal are each reasonably possible. The Supplemental Register is a viable fallback that still confers meaningful benefits (®, USPTO citation against later filers, federal jurisdiction).
3. **Branded merchandise is not cleared** pending review of NATURALIST application SN 98/286,984.
4. **Net recommendation:** proceed only with eyes open — file promptly to secure priority (the application itself is cheap relative to the information it produces), budget for the possibility of an office action or opposition, and hold a fallback name in reserve until the mark clears publication. Alternatively, if the appetite for a GetYourGuide dispute is zero, rebrand now while the cost is lowest.
